# Spine Coherence and Bounded Research Horizons

Use this reference when selecting what to study next, opening or expanding a local
branch, deciding whether a computation is globally relevant, or determining when a
research campaign has enough support to stop and synthesize.

## 1. Coherent research advances bridges, not adjacent detail

A research node can be mathematically correct and still mislead the program if its
special assumptions, local precision, or available computations draw attention away
from the spine's actual missing bridge. Evaluate a candidate continuation by asking:

```text
Which supported upstream object does it consume?
Which downstream claim, observable, or route choice can it change?
What semantic object remains identical across that bridge?
What evidence would make the branch rejoin the spine?
```

A developed branch should do at least one of the following:

- close or falsify a named bridge in the current spine;
- reconstruct a presumption whose failure changes the spine;
- compare alternative routes on the same object or observable;
- produce a checked output consumed by a downstream claim;
- establish a failure boundary that changes the scope or interpretation of the
  global result.

If removing the proposed result from the graph would change no scoped claim,
observable, route decision, or failure boundary, do not develop it as part of the
active spine. It may remain a short side lead or a bounded discovery probe, but it
must not acquire an unlimited chain of descendants.

A technically correct reconstruction of an input label, familiar equation, or
textbook example may still be essential as a regression test. It does not own the
active spine unless it changes a downstream capability. If most of a manuscript is
such infrastructure while the consequential claim appears only at the end,
reclassify the infrastructure as a compact baseline, reference, or companion result
and rebuild the main route around the capability that is actually being tested.

## 2. Give every active branch a spine binding

Before substantial research, record a compact binding:

- **Upstream anchor:** the exact supported construction or unresolved presumption
  consumed by the branch.
- **Bridge question:** the missing semantic transformation or coincidence it must
  construct.
- **Invariant target:** the same state, observable, measure, event, theorem output,
  or physical degree of freedom preserved across the comparison.
- **Downstream effect:** the claim, output, route selection, or reconstruction that
  the result can alter.
- **Special resources:** integrability, low dimension, perturbative scale, symmetry,
  data access, exact solvability, regularization, or other assumptions that may make
  a local success unrepresentative.
- **Rejoin edge:** the exact compact result returned to the spine rather than the
  entire local derivation.

This binding is a semantic contract, not a demand that every interesting question
serve a manuscript immediately. Discovery may explore several candidates, but each
probe should state what ambiguity it discriminates and when that discrimination is
complete.

## 3. Define an internal benchmark and research horizon

A **research horizon** bounds the current inquiry by epistemic scope. It is not an
engineering stage, approval gate, calendar deadline, or claim that the subject is
finished forever.

Define the horizon from the spine itself:

- restricted model or phenomenon class;
- named observable, theorem, prediction, plot, or other output;
- baseline construction and any competing route being tested;
- approximation order, resolution, error, or validity regime that is sufficient
  for the downstream claim;
- assumptions whose removal is inside or outside the present inquiry;
- material and computation required for a decisive check;
- evidence that counts as support, rejection, or reconstruction.

Use the smallest benchmark that can decide the bridge. Do not demand arbitrary
generality, all parameter regimes, every higher-order correction, or a universal
solver when the downstream claim does not consume them.

For a branch `B` joining upstream result `U` to downstream claim `D`, a useful bench
has the shape

```text
U
  -> construct B on a declared model/input
  -> recover invariant target O
  -> compare with baseline or required law
  -> meet error/validity condition epsilon_D
  -> return compact result to D.
```

The tolerance `epsilon_D` may be qualitative when the discriminator is structural,
but it must still state what observation would change the verdict.

## 4. Stop conditions are research results

Stop the active branch and return to the global frontier when any one of these is
established:

1. **Supported bridge:** the benchmark output and checks are met; further refinement
   would not change the downstream claim within scope.
2. **Rejected route:** a counterexample, obstruction, failed recovery, or excessive
   whole-route cost defeats the candidate for the named capability.
3. **Explanatory plateau:** additional detail changes local constants or precision
   but no global claim, observable, route choice, assumption, or failure boundary.
4. **Scope boundary:** progress now requires a new model class, accuracy target,
   empirical material, authority, or user choice outside the declared horizon.
5. **Niche detachment:** the branch remains interesting but has lost its rejoin edge
   to the active spine.
6. **Synthesis readiness:** the supported path already produces the requested
   output, and unresolved alternatives cannot alter its scoped claim or declared
   limitations.

Do not disguise a stop as universal completion. Record:

```text
supported result or obstruction;
presumptions and accepted error;
downstream effect;
why further work is outside the current bench;
the evidence or changed scope that would justify re-entry.
```

A rejected route may be more valuable than a refined niche success when it removes
a misleading global interpretation.

## 5. Audit local progress in the global view

After a material result, unexpected failure, or branch expansion, evaluate the
whole route—not merely the local theorem—along these axes:

| Axis | Global question |
| --- | --- |
| bridge coverage | Which missing spine edge is now supported, rejected, or revised? |
| semantic preservation | Is the same observable or object recovered, or did the target drift? |
| predictive leverage | What can now be computed, proved, plotted, or falsified that could not before? |
| presumption debt | Which symmetry, regularity, scale, integrability, or representation choices paid for the result? |
| computation and recovery cost | Did the full route become cheaper, or was difficulty moved into constructing an inverse, measure, basis, or observable? |
| transfer | Which downstream nodes can reuse the compact result without importing the niche derivation? |
| failure information | What global claim becomes narrower or must be reconstructed? |

Prefer the next branch that addresses the weakest consequential bridge or best
discriminates among live spines. Ease, novelty, available formulas, or local
technical depth are not sufficient selection criteria.

## 6. Guard against misleading special cases and endless continuation

Special models are useful internal benches when their role is explicit:

```text
general spine obligation
  -> special model isolates one bridge
  -> same invariant output is computed
  -> special resource is identified
  -> portable result and nonportable advantage are separated.
```

Do not infer a generic computational method from integrability, a finite matrix,
one perturbative order, one symmetry sector, or a prescribed spectral density.
Conversely, do not reject the global spine because a candidate route fails outside
the regime it was designed to test.

Avoid infinite research by refusing automatic descendants such as “all higher
orders,” “all dimensions,” “all groups,” “all interactions,” or “all observables.”
Open them only when the current spine consumes the added generality or when a
failure indicates that the primitive objects must be reconstructed.

## 7. Report the bounded frontier

At each stop, report three layers separately:

1. **Supported spine:** the coherent path that now reaches the scoped output.
2. **Open bridge:** the weakest remaining connection that can still change the
   global claim.
3. **Parked branches:** meaningful but currently non-discriminating work, with
   explicit re-entry conditions.

This keeps research cumulative without making it append-only, and exploratory
without allowing locally attractive computation to replace the user's global
question.
