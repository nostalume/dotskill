# Research Regimes

Select the regime from the current epistemic state, not from project age or a fixed
delivery stage. A graph may move between regimes when evidence changes.

## 1. Discovery: the spine is unknown

Use discovery when the user has an intent, phenomenon, dissatisfaction, or desired
capability but the central question and argumentative route are not yet stable.
Research explores a hidden space rather than filling a predetermined outline.

Construct a provisional graph that exposes:

- the intent and observable/capability sought;
- candidate objects and modern theory views;
- inherited presumptions and what happens when they fail;
- competing possible spines, not merely adjacent topics;
- materials that support, contradict, or discriminate among them;
- small constructions or counterexamples that clarify the question;
- the frontier: what is now clearer and which ambiguity remains generative.

Nodes may clarify intent, discover vocabulary, gather sources, challenge a premise,
or build a probe. Do not prematurely arrange these into paper sections or pretend a
provisional route is the research thesis.

A spine becomes usable when it names a central capability or claim, the semantic
objects it transforms, the decisive checks, and an output that would count as
success. This is a research result, not a management approval gate.

## 2. Development: the spine is known

Use development when the user has selected the central argumentative route and
wants it rigorized, computed, tested, and arranged into an output such as a paper,
plot, dataset, theorem, or derivation.

Construct a spine-owned graph whose nodes supply exact argument obligations:

- internal construction of every object needed by the spine;
- presumption and theorem-contract boundaries;
- semantic computations witnessing each deduction and equation;
- counterexamples, limiting cases, and equivalence checks;
- isolated heavy computations with compact promoted results;
- synthesis bindings to exact sections, figures, plots, tables, or data products.

The research graph is not the table of contents. The output presents one coherent
supported argument; the graph retains alternatives, sources, failed routes,
computations, and open boundaries.

Do not reopen the entire hidden research space merely because a local proof is
difficult. Return to discovery only when a contradiction, failed prediction, or
unconstructible bridge undermines the spine itself.

## Transition and coexistence

The regimes are not irreversible stages:

```text
unknown intent
  -> discovery probes
  -> candidate spine
  -> development
  -> failed semantic bridge or contradiction
  -> local discovery
  -> revised spine or bounded correction.
```

Different graph regions may be in different regimes. Record which spine is fixed,
which region remains exploratory, and what evidence would change that boundary.

## Output arrangement

When developing a known spine:

1. derive the output's semantic path from supported node results;
2. arrange sections or figures by that path, not source chronology or graph order;
3. keep raw computations and research bookkeeping outside the main argument;
4. make every displayed equation consume a prior construction and produce a named
   semantic result;
5. place alternatives or unresolved branches only where they delimit the claim.

For a plot or dataset, the same rule applies: axes, transformations, estimators,
normalizations, and visual encodings must arise from the research question and have
declared semantics, rather than being chosen as generic presentation defaults.
